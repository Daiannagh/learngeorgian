PRACTICE · A SMALL ROUTINE

A gentle way to practise Georgian between lessons

TEN MINUTES IS A LOVELY START · BY DAIANNA

It’s easy to imagine that learning Georgian needs long study sessions and perfect concentration. Most of us have busy lives, though. A little practice you can return to is often more useful than a grand plan you can’t keep.

Try this ten-minute reset

Minute 1–2: remember one thing

Look back at a word or sentence from your last lesson. Before checking your notes, see what you can remember. It’s okay if you only recall part of it.

Minute 3–5: listen and notice

Listen to that phrase or a short piece of Georgian audio. Don’t try to catch every word. Notice one sound, one familiar word, or the rhythm of the sentence.

Minute 6–8: say it aloud

Repeat the phrase, then change one small detail so it’s about you. Speaking aloud gives your mouth practice and makes it easier to spot what you’re unsure about.

Minute 9–10: leave a note for next time

Write down one question. Maybe a sound feels difficult or you don’t understand why a word changed. Bring that question to your next lesson or explore it together with your teacher.

Let the routine bend

Some days you’ll have ten minutes; some days you won’t. A routine should make learning easier, not turn it into another reason to feel behind. Even one minute spent recalling a phrase keeps the thread alive.

Connect practice to something you already do: after coffee, on the bus, or while waiting for a friend. Keep your notes in one place so you can begin without hunting for materials.

Make it yours

The best practice is connected to your life. If you’re learning Georgian for Batumi, rehearse a greeting you might use at a shop. If you’re learning for family, practise saying something you really want to tell them.

In a one-to-one lesson, we can shape practice around the things you hope to say. Or start with three useful Georgian phrases.
